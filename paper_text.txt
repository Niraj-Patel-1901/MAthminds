
Dataset Used
The IM2LATEX-100K dataset is a large-scale, prebuilt benchmark dataset designed for image-to-LaTeX generation tasks [13]. It contains approximately 100,000 paired samples, where each sample consists of a rendered mathematical formula image and its corresponding LaTeX source code.

LimitationsDespite demonstrating the effectiveness of a digital doubt-solving platform for Engineering Mathematics (EM1–EM4), the current system has certain limitations. The solution generation mechanism is primarily rule-based and relies on predefined solution paths, which restricts its ability to handle unseen or highly complex problem variations. While this approach ensures correctness and clarity for commonly encountered syllabus-based questions, it limits scalability across diverse mathematical domains. Additionally, the system’s performance is influenced by the clarity and quality of the input questions, which may affect the interpretation of complex multi-line derivations. The platform also lacks adaptive learning capabilities, meaning it does not dynamically adjust problem difficulty or explanations based on individual learner performance. Furthermore, evaluation is currently limited to qualitative observations and sample problem testing, without large-scale user studies or statistical learning outcome analysis.                                         Future ScopeThe proposed system offers significant potential for future enhancements. One key extension is the integration of intelligent solution engines capable of dynamically solving unseen Engineering Mathematics problems using symbolic reasoning or machine learning models. Adaptive learning mechanisms can be introduced to personalize content based on a student’s strengths, weaknesses, and learning pace. Expanding the problem database to include advanced numerical methods, higher-order differential equations, and real-world engineering applications would further increase academic relevance. Additionally, incorporating analytics-driven feedback and performance dashboards can help students and educators track progress more effectively. The platform can also be extended to mobile and cloud-based environments, enabling wider accessibility and real-time academic support. With these enhancements, the system can evolve into a comprehensive intelligent tutoring solution for engineering education.                                          ConclusionThe project effectively addresses one of the major challenges faced by engineering students understanding and solving complex problems in Engineering Mathematics (EM1–EM4). Many students struggle to grasp fundamental concepts and step-by-step solution methods, often relying solely on final answers without clarity on the reasoning behind them. This platform bridges that gap by providing detailed, concept-oriented explanations that guide learners through each stage of problem solving. By offering topic-wise modules and personalized doubt-solving support, it transforms the traditional learning experience into an interactive and self-paced process. The system helps students strengthen their conceptual understanding, build confidence in applying mathematical methods, and reduce academic dependency on external coaching or textbooks.
